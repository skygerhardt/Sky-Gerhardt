# Perfect Competition — Decision Memo

## Recommendation

I recommend planting 10 beds of tomatoes, 20 beds of carrots, and 30 beds of mesclun. This mix uses 60 of the farm's 64 available beds and produces approximately $42.8K in season profit. Even though tomatoes bring in the most revenue per bed, the model shows that planting more than 10 is not worth it because the marginal cost of the 11th tomato bed is higher than its $8,800 price.

## Why

The model shows that the 10th tomato bed still makes economic sense because its marginal cost is about $8,248, which is below the $8,800 revenue it produces. The 11th bed has a marginal cost of about $9,390, so the farm should stop at 10.

Carrots and mesclun are different because both reach their maximum allowed number of beds. Carrots stop at 20 beds and mesclun stops at 30 beds because of their crop caps, not because the next beds are no longer profitable.

## The judgment call

If the farm could expand one crop-specific limit, I would increase the carrot cap first. The model shows that allowing one additional carrot bed would increase profit by about $353, compared with about $247 for one additional mesclun bed.

I would not recommend buying additional general farmland right now. The current plan only uses 60 of the 64 available beds, so the farm already has four unused beds. Increasing the crop-specific capacity for carrots would be more valuable than simply adding more land.

## What would change my answer

The recommendation would change if crop prices, labor costs, or the crop limits changed significantly. I would especially watch the tomato price because the current stopping point is determined by where tomato marginal cost crosses the $8,800 market price.
